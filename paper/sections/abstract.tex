We study, entirely in simulation, whether a BB-8 style spherical robot can follow a person by
listening to chirps from their phone, using a four-microphone INMP441-class array with a 5\,cm
radius. In the 17--23\,kHz band such an array is spatially aliased and multipath is strong. We
built a GPU acoustic renderer with image-source multipath and two front-end models, together
with a pendulum-cart sphere model on six terrains. On top of these we compare a deep
multichannel localizer (\TrainFullParams{} parameters, first layer initialised to the SRP-PHAT
steering of the array) with GCC-PHAT and SRP-PHAT, and three controller families: the deep
localizer with a Kalman tracker and pursuit, a cascaded PID with a learned terrain residual,
and a PPO policy. On \NTestSet{} test recordings the deep localizer reaches a bearing MAE of
\LocdlfullBMAE$^\circ$ (median \LocdlfullBMed$^\circ$) against \LocsrpphatBMAE$^\circ$
(\LocsrpphatBMed$^\circ$) for SRP-PHAT, and a range MAE of \LocdlfullRMAE\,m against
\LocsrpphatRMAE\,m. Without phase it is at chance (\LocdlmagBMAE$^\circ$); without multipath in
training it reaches \LocdlanechoicBMAE$^\circ$. In \CLAusN{} closed-loop episodes per terrain
on the ideal channel, pooled follow success is \CLAusoraclesucc\% for an oracle localizer,
\CLAusgcctracksucc\% with GCC-PHAT, \CLAusdltracksucc\% with the deep localizer,
\CLAusdlpidsucc\% for DL + PID (which tumbles), \CLAusdlpidressucc\% with the residual, and
\CLAusrlsucc\% for PPO, the only controller that handles slopes. With a realistic front end and
a 17--19.5\,kHz chirp, the corresponding rates are \CLBfbgcctracksucc\%, \CLBfbdltracksucc\%
and \CLBfbrlsucc\% for GCC-PHAT, DL + tracker and PPO. We release the simulator, models,
ESP32 firmware and a companion app. No hardware experiment was run.
